The "Show as a display" options are compact and separate the Mac's display from how it's streamed.

## Changed
- **Three groups instead of a list of radio buttons**, each a row of buttons (Material 3 Expressive's connected button groups). At the window's default size everything fits without scrolling.
  - **Where:** full screen or a window. The display to use only appears for full screen, and becomes a list with more than four displays.
  - **The Mac's display:** its size (match the screen or window, 4K, 1440p or 1080p) and its refresh rate. A line under it sums up what the Mac sees, e.g. "A 3840×2160 display at 120 Hz".
  - **Stream:** Adaptive, Sharpest, Balanced or Fastest. With Adaptive, a "While things move" row picks 1080p (quicker) or 1440p (sharper). A line under it says what you'll actually see, e.g. "You'll see 3840×2160 when still and 1920×1080 while things move, at up to 120 fps".
- **Every refresh rate can be chosen.** The Mac's display runs at the rate you pick. If the Mac can't encode that many frames at the size it sends (full 4K manages about 60 a second), the stream sends as many as it can. The Stream line says so, and names a quality that keeps up. Before, the display was capped too, and those rates were greyed out.

## Updating
Either machine can take this version on its own: it still connects to 0.3.0-alpha.18 and later. The display only runs faster than the stream once both have it; until then it's capped as before.

On the Mac, quit Legato, install the new version, then run:
```sh
xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Legato.app
tccutil reset Accessibility io.github.tvolk131.legato
tccutil reset ListenEvent io.github.tvolk131.legato
tccutil reset ScreenCapture io.github.tvolk131.legato
```
Then open Legato and allow the prompts.
